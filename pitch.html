Open-source · self-hosted · built by a parent

Genkan.

A home network that hands parents back the off switch.

It turns any always-on computer into the gateway for a separate kids’ network, so screen time, games and YouTube answer to you, from your phone, in plain words. Music, homework and talking to Mum and Dad stay free.

The problem every parent knows

The doom-scroll, the “one more Roblox game”, the YouTube hole that swallows a Saturday. The apps are built by professionals to defeat a child’s self-control, and the off switch is buried in ten different settings menus. Worse, one blunt filter can’t be right for an 11, a 14 and a 16-year-old at once. Genkan puts the off switch in one place, and makes it fit each kid.

What it does

Everything below is a tap on your phone, or a sentence to the household assistant: “turn off Ben’s internet”, “kill gaming”, “dinner”.

PER KID

Age-tiered, not one-size-fits-all

The 11-year-old gets tight rails; the 16-year-old gets a light touch and honesty. Each child, their own rules.

TIMED

A clock that only counts what matters

Games and YouTube are metered against a daily budget. Music, chess, homework and messaging you are never timed. Singing in the shower to Spotify is free.

GAMING OFF

Switch off one thing, not everything

Kill Roblox, Fortnite and Steam while chess, music and Google Docs keep working. Or flip the whole internet off. Or just video and scrolling.

STUDY MODE

Homework without the rabbit holes

One switch: schoolwork sites and music stay on, games and video go dark.

TIME’S UP

Earn it back, don’t just get blocked

When the time runs out, their screen shows a friendly page: “do the dishes for +30 minutes, then come see Dad.” Chores and study buy time back.

SAFER

Filtering with a conscience

Adult, gambling and self-harm content blocked, SafeSearch forced. And youth help lines stay reachable even when a child is switched off.

WALLED OFF

Guests can’t touch your home

Visitors get internet on the kids’ island, fully sealed off from your own computers and files.

What it won’t pretend to do

The honesty that most parental-control ads skip.

  • It can’t read inside apps. Bullying in Snapchat or Instagram DMs is encrypted and invisible to any network tool. That needs a different kind of app, and we’ll point you to one.
  • It can’t touch mobile data. Cellular never crosses your home network. Google Family Link is the lever there, and Genkan is built to run happily alongside it.
  • A determined teenager can get around it — so we made that the point. A built-in household “bug bounty” rewards kids for finding a bypass and showing you how, turning the arms race into a genuine lesson in how networks work.

Why “Genkan”?

The genkan is the floor of a fireplace — the one warm spot a family once gathered around every evening.

Over centuries the word came to mean home and togetherness itself (it’s a close cousin of heart). It felt right for a project whose whole aim is to pull a family back from separate screens in separate rooms, toward the warm centre again. Using a bit of technology to undo what technology did to family evenings.

The ask

Help make it awesome

Right now this is one dad and a home full of test subjects. It gets genuinely good when other parents and tinkerers pile in, because every home, every router and every kid is different. If any of this speaks to you, there’s a job for you whether or not you write code:

  • 01
    Try it on your gear. Different routers and access points need testing. Break it, tell us how.
  • 02
    Sharpen the parenting. What’s a fair budget? When should the 16-year-old’s rules loosen? This is as much a parenting question as a technical one.
  • 03
    Grow the lists. Help decide what counts as “gaming” vs “homework” vs “just music”, and keep it current.
  • 04
    Write, translate, design. Setup guides a non-technical parent can actually follow.

Genkan is a working title — naming it is the first thing we’ll do together.
