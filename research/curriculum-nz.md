# The New Zealand curriculum, mapped for quiz banks

Research note for HEARTH's learn-to-earn system. Written August 2026.
Purpose: understand how NZ schooling is structured so quiz banks land at
the right level for real kids (the reference family has kids aged 11, 14
and 16, roughly Years 7, 10 and 12), and plan bank coverage honestly:
some subjects quiz well, some do not.

## 1. How NZ schooling is structured

### Years, ages and curriculum levels

School runs Year 1 to Year 13, starting around age 5. The New Zealand
Curriculum (NZC) describes progress in 8 curriculum levels that map
loosely onto year levels. The mapping deliberately overlaps because kids
progress at different speeds, but the working approximation is:

| Curriculum level | Typical years | Typical ages |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1 | Y1-2 | 5-7 |
| Level 2 | Y3-4 | 7-9 |
| Level 3 | Y5-6 | 9-11 |
| Level 4 | Y7-8 | 11-13 |
| Level 5 | Y9-10 | 13-15 |
| Level 6 | Y11 | 15-16 (NCEA Level 1) |
| Level 7 | Y12 | 16-17 (NCEA Level 2) |
| Level 8 | Y13 | 17-18 (NCEA Level 3) |

The first ten years are broad and common to everyone. From Year 11 kids
specialise into subjects and national qualifications take over.

### Learning areas

The NZC has eight learning areas:

1. English
2. Mathematics and statistics
3. Science
4. Social sciences (including Aotearoa New Zealand's Histories,
   compulsory content since 2023)
5. Technology
6. The arts
7. Health and physical education
8. Learning languages (including te reo Māori)

Te reo Māori and tikanga Māori also thread through everything, not just
the languages area.

### The refresh: Te Mātaiaho

The curriculum is mid-refresh. Te Mātaiaho, the refreshed New Zealand
Curriculum, restructures Years 0-10 around five phases of learning
(Years 0-3, 4-6, 7-8, 9-10, 11-13) and describes each phase with an
"Understand, Know, Do" model: big ideas (understand), content and
concepts (know), and skills and practices (do). It is deliberately more
knowledge-rich and more specific about what should be taught in which
year than the 2007 NZC was.

Status as of late August 2026:

- Final Years 0-10 content is out for English and mathematics and
  statistics, with structured literacy and numeracy approaches and
  twice-yearly progress checks.
- Draft content for the remaining learning areas was released for
  consultation (feedback ran to April 2026), with the full updated
  national curriculum planned for publication by September 2026.
- The Arts, Technology and Learning Languages content becomes required
  from Term 1 2028.
- Schools are encouraged to run the full new Year 9 curriculum from
  2026, because that cohort will be the first into the new senior
  qualifications from 2028.
- The old framework formally remains in effect until 1 January 2027.

Practical upshot for HEARTH: pin banks to topics and levels, not to
either curriculum document. Facts like "fractions by the end of the
Years 4-6 phase" survive the refresh; page references do not.

### NCEA today (and its replacement)

NCEA (National Certificate of Educational Achievement) is the current
senior qualification, at Levels 1, 2 and 3, roughly Years 11, 12, 13.
Key mechanics:

- Credits. Each standard (a chunk of assessed work) is worth credits.
  A level needs 80 credits (Levels 2 and 3: 60 at that level plus 20
  from anywhere).
- Standards are internal (assessed by the school through the year) or
  external (end-of-year exams). Grades: Not Achieved, Achieved, Merit,
  Excellence. Certificates can be endorsed with Merit or Excellence.
- The literacy and numeracy corequisite: 20 credits (10 literacy, 10
  numeracy), commonly earned through online common assessment
  activities. Needed for any NCEA level.
- Level 1 is optional; a number of schools skip it and start
  qualifications at Level 2.
- University Entrance sits on top of Level 3: 14 credits in each of
  three approved subjects, plus UE literacy and numeracy requirements.

Big caveat: NCEA is being replaced. The announced structure is a
foundational award in Year 11 (from 2028), the New Zealand Certificate
of Education (NZCE, Year 12, from 2029) and the New Zealand Advanced
Certificate of Education (NZACE, Year 13, from 2030), sitting on new
Year 11-13 subject curricula. Kids currently in NCEA finish under NCEA.
For HEARTH this mostly changes labels, not content: "NCEA Level 1
Science" banks should be tagged by topic (genetics, mechanics, acids
and bases) so they survive the rename.

## 2. Which subjects suit multiple-choice banks, honestly

Multiple choice tests recall and recognition. It is genuinely good at
facts, vocabulary, conventions, rules and worked micro-problems. It is
bad at composition, argument, craft and performance. Pretending
otherwise produces banks that feel like homework and teach little.

### Quizzes work well

- Mathematics and statistics: number facts, times tables, fractions,
  percentages, order of operations, unit conversion, reading graphs,
  probability basics. Single correct answers, easy to verify, easy to
  generate distractors from common mistakes. The best fit in the whole
  curriculum.
- Science (knowledge strands): states of matter, cells, body systems,
  circuits, forces, the periodic table, astronomy, NZ ecology (native
  birds, pest species). Factual cores quiz beautifully.
- Social sciences (facts and civics): geography, capitals, NZ
  government basics (MMP, electorates, how laws pass), key dates and
  events in NZ history (Te Tiriti o Waitangi 1840, women's suffrage
  1893).
- English (mechanics only): spelling (NZ English), punctuation rules,
  parts of speech, vocabulary, literary term definitions.
- Learning languages (vocabulary and forms): te reo Māori kupu,
  colours, numbers, days; French or Japanese vocab. Recognition-level
  only, and see the te ao Māori caveat below.
- Practical life knowledge that schools brush past: the Road Code
  (already a bank), first aid basics, financial literacy (interest,
  KiwiSaver basics, budgeting), typing and computing fundamentals.

### Quizzes work partly, design with care

- Reading comprehension: possible, but each question needs a passage,
  which strains the format. Keep passages to two or three sentences.
- Science (investigation strand): "which is the fair test" style
  questions work; actual practical skills do not.
- Maths at NCEA level: multi-step problems have single answers but the
  working is the point. Quiz the checkpoints (which formula, first
  step, estimate of the answer), not whole excellence-level problems.
- History and social studies interpretation: stick to what happened
  and when; "why it mattered" invites contested single answers, which
  is exactly what multiple choice does worst.

### Quizzes do not work, do not force it

- Essay writing, creative writing, speech craft. The whole skill is
  production, not recognition.
- Practical assessment: PE performance, technology builds, art
  portfolios, lab technique, drama and music performance.
- The full licence driving test equivalent of any subject: anything
  where the assessment is "do the thing in front of an assessor".
- Interpretation of literature beyond term definitions.

For these, HEARTH should stay a motivator, not an assessor: a parent
can still credit minutes through the existing chores path ("wrote a
story", "practised guitar for 30 minutes") with `kidnet earn`.

### Te ao Māori content needs care, not avoidance

Te reo Māori vocabulary, Matariki, pūrākau and NZ histories belong in
the banks: they are core curriculum and core to growing up here. But:

- Facts about te ao Māori are not all pan-Māori. Iwi differ (some mark
  the new year by Puanga rather than Matariki). Say "many iwi" or "in
  many traditions" rather than flattening.
- Macrons are not optional: Taupō, Whangārei, Waitematā, Māori.
- Any bank teaching te reo itself ships only after review by a fluent
  speaker (already house policy in learn-to-earn.md, `needs_review`
  flag). Getting the reo right beats shipping fast.
- Do not quiz tapu or contested material (details of specific iwi
  histories, karakia) from a general-knowledge stance.

## 3. Phased plan for bank coverage

Sequenced for the reference family (11, 14, 16) first, younger bands
filled in later since the format already has Y1-6 friendly banks.

### Phase A, now: Y7-8 (curriculum levels 3-4) and Y9-10 (levels 4-5)

The 11 and 14 year olds. Highest value per bank because both kids can
share several of them.

- Maths: times tables to 12 (exists), fractions and decimals,
  percentages, order of operations, basic algebra (Y9-10).
- Science: science basics (exists), astronomy (this batch), NZ native
  wildlife and ecology, human body systems, states of matter and
  simple chemistry.
- Social sciences: NZ geography (exists), world capitals and flags
  (exist), NZ history key events, civics and MMP basics.
- English mechanics: NZ English spelling bee, punctuation and grammar.
- Te reo Māori basics: drafted, held for fluent review before shipping.
- General smarts: chess basics (this batch), typing, internet safety.

### Phase B, now: NCEA years / Y11-13 (levels 6-8)

The 16 year old. Tag by topic so the NCEA to NZCE rename does not
invalidate anything.

- Road Code learner theory (this batch): the single most motivating
  bank for a 16 year old, and genuinely examinable, since the real
  learner theory test is itself 35 multiple-choice questions.
- NCEA Level 1-2 science topics: mechanics formulas and concepts,
  atoms and the periodic table, genetics vocabulary.
- Numeracy and literacy corequisite practice: the corequisite is
  assessed in exactly this recognition style, so banks map well.
- Financial literacy: interest, tax basics (PAYE, GST), KiwiSaver,
  first job rights. Not deeply examined at school, very examinable
  here, immediately useful.
- First aid and safety basics.

### Phase C, later: Y1-6 (levels 1-3)

For younger visitors, cousins and other families adopting HEARTH.

- Number bonds and early times tables, `suggested_age_min` 6-8.
- Reading words and sight vocabulary, phonics-consistent spelling.
- Sorting, shapes, clocks and calendars, coins.
- NZ animals and Matariki for juniors (simpler than the astronomy
  bank).
- Keep rounds short and pass marks kind at this band; the portal's
  `suggested_age_min` already handles who sees what first.

### Phase D, ongoing: maintenance rules

- Re-verify rule-based banks (Road Code, financial, civics) once a
  year; NZ road and licensing rules are changing in January 2027, and
  tax figures move most Budgets.
- Retire or re-tag NCEA-labelled banks as the NZCE/NZACE rollout lands
  (2028-2030).
- Watch the final Te Mātaiaho publication (due September 2026) for the
  Year 11-13 subject list and align Phase B topics to it.
- Per-question stats (roadmap in learn-to-earn.md) tell us which
  questions are broken; everyone failing a question means the question
  is wrong, not the kids.
